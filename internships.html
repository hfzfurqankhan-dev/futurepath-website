Find and land an internship

A practical guide to finding and landing an internship

  • A clean one-page CV
  • Proof of real work
  • Where to look and how to prepare

An internship is your first real bridge between study and work. A verified listings tool is on our roadmap; until then, here’s how to find one and make it count.

How to land an internship

Prepare a CV

One clean page: education, skills, any projects or volunteer work. No experience yet? Lead with projects.

Build proof

A GitHub, portfolio or social page showing real work beats any claim on a CV.

Where to look

Company career pages, LinkedIn, university placement offices and trusted job boards.

Prepare for interviews

Research the company, rehearse common questions, and have 2–3 questions ready to ask.

What to expect

Most internships last 6–12 weeks and pay a small stipend (some are unpaid). You’ll likely start with smaller tasks and earn more responsibility as you prove yourself. The real prize is experience, references and a clearer sense of what you want.

Watch out. Never pay for an internship, and be cautious of long unpaid “internships” that use you as free labour without teaching you anything. A good internship teaches you and respects your time.
